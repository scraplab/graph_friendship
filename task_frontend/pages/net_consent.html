Study Information


Please read the following information carefully before deciding whether to participate in this experiment.


Participation is voluntary.

It is your choice whether or not to participate in this research. If you choose to participate, you may change your mind and leave the study at any time. Refusal to participate or stopping your participation will involve no penalty or loss of benefits to which you are otherwise entitled. If at any time before the completion of the study you close the experiment window or tab, this will be treated as withdrawn consent and any data you have provided will be discarded.

What is the purpose of this research?

The purpose of this research is to understand what factors influence people's decisions about who to form social connections with.

How long will I take part in this research?

Your participation will last about five minutes. You will respond to 30 trials in the main task.

What can I expect if I take part in this research?

As a participant, you will provide demographic information and then complete a 30-trial task. On each trial, you will be shown a naturalistically structured social network, with circles representing people. You will be asked to make a new connection between yourself and someone else. Your goal will be to improve your own social status in the network. At the end of the study you will rate your experience in the study and then be shown your results.

What are the risks and possible discomforts?

If you choose to participate, we anticipate that you will experience no greater risk or discomfort than you would experience in normal use of a computer for the same period of time.

Are there any benefits from being in this research study?

You will receive a visualization reflecting the degree to which you use rely on different forms of centrality to inform your decisions about forming new social connections. You will also see the average degree to which other participants make use of the same information. You will have the opporunity to learn more about network centarlity using an interactive demo.

Will I be compensated for participating in this research?

This is a purely volunteer study. Your only compensation will be the benefits described above: the results from the social network task.

If I take part in this research, how will my privacy be protected? What happens to the information you collect?

The data we collect from this study will not be identified by any names, email addresses, or IP addresses. If you have a user account with us and are currently signed in, your results will be linked to this account and we may combine data across multiple experiments for research purposes. Note, however, that identifying account information, such as your email address, will remain encrypted to prevent anyone from connecting your identity to the data you provide. Aggregate data from this study may be presented in scientific publications or talks. Non-identifiable data from this study may be shared publicly to further scientific research. Great care will be taken to ensure that it is not possible to recover individual identities from shared data.

If I have any questions, concerns or complaints about this research study, who can I talk to?

The researcher for this study is Mark Thornton, who can be reached at researcher@mysocialbrain.org if you have questions, concerns, or complaints, if you would like to talk to the research team, if you think the research has harmed you, or if you wish to withdraw from the study.

MySocialBrain.org has received ethics approval from the Committee for the Protection of Human Subjects at Dartmouth College. They can be reached by phone at (603) 646-6482, or by email at cphs@dartmouth.edu, if your questions, concerns, or complaints are not being answered by the research team, if you cannot reach the research team, if you want to talk to someone besides the research team, or if you have questions about your rights as a research participant.
